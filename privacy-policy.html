Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-15

This Privacy Policy explains how PixelBrick (“we,” “us,” or “our”) handles information in the TorchPhoto app and on torchphoto.com. It covers local media features, diagnostic reporting, support requests, and your privacy choices.

Your Photos and Identity

Your photos, videos, and server login credentials are not uploaded to PixelBrick as diagnostic data. TorchPhoto connects your device directly to the servers you configure. You do not need to create a PixelBrick account to browse your media.

Diagnostic reporting does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, or contacts. It is intended to help us understand technical problems, not to identify you personally or build an advertising profile. Diagnostic reports are designed to exclude your media contents, server addresses, account credentials, passwords, original filenames and folder paths, captions, and embedded GPS coordinates.

Technical reports do include an app-installation identifier, and our hosting providers process network connection information. If you email us, we also receive your sender information and anything you choose to include. These details are explained below; we do not describe all information handled by the service as anonymous.

Media, Server Connections, and Local Storage

TorchPhoto accesses your chosen servers using FTP, SFTP, SMB, or WebDAV. Browsing, uploading, and downloading media take place between your device and those servers, without routing your media through PixelBrick’s diagnostic service.

Server addresses, ports, usernames, connection settings, and selected paths are stored on your device for those connections. Passwords and supported SFTP private-key credentials use the device’s protected credential storage.

The app stores thumbnails, downloaded or prefetched media, cached metadata, captions, folder tags, favorites, preferences, and upload history locally to provide its features. Cached metadata can include information already embedded in your files, such as capture dates and locations. Cache controls, storage limits, and feature-specific cleanup determine how long local copies remain.

If you choose to export, share, or back up data, the selected information goes to the destination or recipient you choose. Backup files may contain thumbnails, file paths, server details, metadata, settings, and local diagnostic records. Wi-Fi Gallery Share makes the gallery you select available to other devices on the local network while you enable that feature. These user-directed actions are separate from diagnostic reporting.

Device Permissions and Apple Services

TorchPhoto uses photo-library access for the media actions you request, including selecting or preparing uploads, saving media, and reading a server QR code from an image. It uses the camera for QR scanning, local-network access for server connections and local sharing, and music-library access for selected slideshow music. You can manage these permissions in iOS Settings; denying a permission may prevent the corresponding feature from working.

The app does not request your device’s current GPS location. When you use location-related media features, it may use coordinates already embedded in a photo or video. Apple Maps and geocoding services may process those coordinates to provide maps and place information.

Apple handles in-app purchases and subscriptions through StoreKit. TorchPhoto uses purchase and entitlement status to enable the features you purchased; PixelBrick does not receive your payment-card details. Apple may also provide system crash and performance diagnostics under its platform services and applicable settings. Apple’s own analytics sharing is separate from TorchPhoto’s diagnostic-reporting setting. See Apple’s privacy information.

Technical Diagnostics and Troubleshooting

TorchPhoto records technical activity and errors on your device to investigate crashes, interruptions, upload failures, connection problems, and performance or storage issues. Records can include:

  • App version and build, operating-system version, device model or family, and relevant feature or permission settings.
  • Error codes and categories, occurrence times and counts, technical stack information, and the operation or stage involved.
  • Network connection type and protocol, memory and storage measurements, cache usage, transfer progress, and item counts.
  • Technical media characteristics such as format, file size, dimensions, or duration, and generated reference values used to correlate items or operations without including their original names.

The app limits diagnostic fields and filters technical text to remove recognizable addresses, paths, filenames, and credentials. It does not attach the contents of your photos or videos to diagnostic reports. PixelBrick reviews technical reports remotely to investigate abnormal behavior and crashes that cause the app to close, including issues raised in user feedback. Diagnostic information is used only to identify the causes of technical problems, develop corrections, and verify those corrections. Remote review is limited to the diagnostic information received. We do not sell these reports or use them for advertising or tracking you across other companies’ apps and websites.

Diagnostic reporting is enabled by default in current app versions. When enabled, the app sends eligible abnormal-event and crash records to PixelBrick’s diagnostic service hosted on Cloudflare without requiring a separate feedback email or a send action for each report. Enabling reporting can also send eligible records already stored on your device; records awaiting delivery may be retried.

The app creates a random credential for its diagnostic installation. The server stores a derived identifier to associate reports from that installation and prevent duplicate processing. This is not your name, Apple ID, server username, or advertising identifier, but it allows reports from the same installation to be linked.

Diagnostic uploads use HTTPS. Detailed diagnostic evidence is additionally encrypted on the device for access by PixelBrick. Basic report information, such as error category, app version, timestamps, and occurrence counts, is stored separately so the diagnostic service can organize reports. Cloudflare also processes network information, such as IP addresses and request information, to deliver and protect the service. See Cloudflare’s Privacy Policy.

Your Diagnostic Choices

You can turn off Automatic error reporting in the app’s Diagnostics screen. In current versions, open Settings, tap the Settings title at the top four times, then tap Reset Tips once at the bottom to open Diagnostics. The reporting control is available to both Free and PRO users.

Turning reporting off stops further diagnostic uploads; it does not disable photo browsing, uploads to your own server, or other features available with your plan. Local diagnostic recording continues. Turning reporting off does not recall a report already delivered or delete records already held by the service.

You can use Reset Diagnostics on the same screen to clear local diagnostic history. This does not turn off reporting or delete server copies. If you need help locating the control or want to request deletion of server records, contact us using the email address below.

Feedback and Support

If you send us an email, we receive your email address, any sender name supplied by your email service, your message, and any attachments you include. A diagnostic attachment may include local records, language or locale, relevant settings, cache measurements, and subscription-status information to help us investigate the issue. Email attachments and the app’s diagnostic-reporting feature are separate ways of providing diagnostic information.

We use support information to respond to your request and investigate or resolve the reported problem. Only include screenshots, files, or other information you want to share with us. Please do not send server passwords, private keys, or unrelated personal information. Support correspondence is handled through your email provider and our support email service.

Retention and Deletion

On your device: routine diagnostic activity expires after seven days. Abnormal records are grouped by cause and are not deleted solely because of their age. Local diagnostic storage is bounded to approximately 2 MB, and older detail may be compacted to stay within that limit. Successful reporting does not remove the local record; you can clear local history with Reset Diagnostics.

On the diagnostic service: our retention policy is to keep reports only for investigating the relevant technical problem, developing a correction, and completing necessary verification. Once those tasks are complete and the reports are no longer needed for that purpose, we delete the reports and developer-held working copies without undue delay. This deletion is performed manually following review, not through a preset expiry schedule. Turning off reporting, clearing local history, or uninstalling the app does not itself delete reports already received by the service.

Support messages: correspondence and attachments may be retained for handling your request, related follow-up, and applicable legal obligations. Diagnostic attachments follow the troubleshooting retention policy above. You can request deletion of support information or diagnostic reports by emailing us. Because reports sent through the diagnostic service are not indexed by your name or email address, we may need a report reference, approximate event date, or other limited information to locate your records and verify the request. We will explain any limitation that prevents us from identifying or deleting particular records.

Deleting app data does not delete originals on your servers, items saved in Photos, or exported backups. Those copies remain under the controls of their respective locations and providers.

Service Providers and Website Visits

We use Cloudflare to receive and store diagnostic reports, GitHub Pages to host this website, and email services to handle support correspondence. Providers process information needed to deliver their services under their applicable terms and privacy protections. Information may also be disclosed where required by law or necessary to address security incidents and protect legal rights.

Visiting torchphoto.com sends normal web-request information to its hosting and asset providers. GitHub states that GitHub Pages logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes. Pages may also load external assets, such as stylesheets from cdnjs, which creates requests to that provider. See the GitHub Privacy Statement and Cloudflare’s Privacy Policy.

Our service providers operate internationally, so support, diagnostic, and website information may be processed in countries other than your own. Their policies describe their processing locations and applicable safeguards. Servers and sharing destinations that you configure are governed by your arrangements with those providers.

Security

We use protections appropriate to the data involved, including protected credential storage on the device, HTTPS for diagnostic transfers, additional encryption for detailed diagnostic evidence, and restricted access to diagnostic administration. No storage or transmission method can guarantee absolute security. The security of connections to your own servers depends on the protocol and server configuration you select.

Your Privacy Rights

Depending on your location and the information involved, you may have rights to request access, correction, deletion, restriction, or portability of your personal information, to object to processing, or to withdraw consent where processing relies on consent. You may also have the right to complain to your local data-protection authority. Contact us to exercise applicable rights; we may request limited information needed to verify and fulfil your request.

This policy describes data handling. Reading it or continuing to use the app does not, by itself, replace any consent required by applicable law or platform rules.

Children

TorchPhoto is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly seek their personal information. If you believe a child has provided personal information to us, contact us so we can investigate and address the request, including deletion where appropriate.

Changes to This Policy

We may update this policy to reflect changes to the app, our services, or applicable requirements. We will update the date on this page and provide additional notice or obtain consent where required.
